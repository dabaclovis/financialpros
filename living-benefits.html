Living benefits: read the rider, not just the name

Some life policies allow part of a death benefit to be accessed during the insured person's lifetime if contract conditions are met. The details matter.

Published by TrueVine Financial. This is general U.S. financial education, not an individual recommendation. See our editorial standards.

What an accelerated death benefit rider may do

An accelerated death benefit rider may let an eligible policy owner request some of a life insurance death benefit before the insured person dies after a qualifying event. Depending on the contract, qualifying events may involve a terminal illness, chronic illness, or another specified condition. Some policies include a rider and others offer it as an optional feature; terms are not uniform.

A rider is not necessarily a separate pool of money. A benefit paid early can reduce the remaining death benefit available to beneficiaries, sometimes by more than the amount received after accounting for discounting, interest, or fees. The contract explains how the payment is calculated.

Check the conditions and trade-offs

  • Which diagnoses or care needs qualify, and what proof or certification is required?
  • Are there waiting periods, minimums, maximums, or limits on how often a request can be made?
  • How does the insurer calculate the amount offered, and are administrative charges or discounts applied?
  • How will taking a benefit affect the policy's remaining death benefit, cash value, premiums, or other riders?
  • Could a payment affect taxes, public benefits, or eligibility for needs-based assistance?

Ask the insurer for the rider form and a personalized explanation of how a hypothetical claim would affect the policy and its beneficiaries. Do not assume an advertised maximum is the amount that would be payable in a particular situation.

Not the same as disability income insurance

An accelerated death benefit is tied to a life insurance contract and its qualifying conditions. Disability income insurance, when available and applicable, is designed under separate contract terms to replace some income when a covered disability prevents work. Neither type automatically covers every illness, injury, expense, or period away from work. Compare definitions, exclusions, waiting periods, benefit duration, and claim requirements in each contract.
