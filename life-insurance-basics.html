Life insurance basics: start with the purpose

Understand the broad differences between coverage types, then compare the actual contract—not just a label or headline price.

Published by TrueVine Financial. This page is general U.S. financial education, not an individual recommendation. See our editorial standards.

What life insurance is designed to do

Life insurance is a contract between an owner and an insurer. If the insured person dies while the policy is in force and the claim meets the contract terms, the insurer pays a benefit to the named beneficiary or beneficiaries. Coverage, eligibility, exclusions, and payment depend on the policy and applicable law.

People often consider coverage to help dependents manage ongoing costs, repay obligations, or meet another stated goal. Start by listing the people and expenses that could be affected, the time period involved, and resources that would actually be available. The family protection guide's worked example shows one way to organize those assumptions; it is not a recommended coverage amount.

Term and permanent coverage

Term life insurance

Term insurance generally provides a stated death benefit for a defined period, subject to the contract remaining in force. Review the level-premium period, what happens when it ends, renewal or conversion options, and the cost of any renewal. A term policy typically does not build cash value.

Permanent life insurance

Permanent policies are designed to provide coverage beyond a fixed term when contract requirements are met. Some types include cash-value features, but premiums, expenses, guarantees, and access rules vary. A policy illustration is not a promise of non-guaranteed results; ask the insurer to identify guaranteed values and assumptions separately.

These are broad categories, not a substitute for reading the specific policy. Features and names vary among insurers and products.

How to compare options

  • Check who is insured, who owns the policy, and who is named as beneficiary.
  • Compare the benefit, premium schedule, duration, exclusions, and conditions for keeping coverage active.
  • Ask whether premiums or benefits can change and what happens if a payment is missed.
  • For a policy with cash value, request an explanation of charges, loans, withdrawals, and possible effects on coverage.
  • Ask about the professional's license, compensation, and which insurers or products are available to them.

Questions to ask before applying

Ask for the policy form and consumer disclosures, and take time to understand the exclusions and any free-look or cancellation period that applies in your state. An application is subject to underwriting; an estimate or illustration does not establish coverage. Coverage begins only as specified in the issued contract.

Continue with living-benefit riders, review ways to prepare for an income interruption, or use the full family protection guide.

Do not rely on a website to select coverage for your circumstances. Verify licensing with the relevant state insurance department and discuss contract, tax, or legal questions with appropriately qualified professionals.
