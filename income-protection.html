Prepare for an interruption in income

A simple household inventory can help you understand which bills continue, what resources may be available, and which questions to ask about coverage.

Published by TrueVine Financial. This is general U.S. financial education, not an individual recommendation. See our editorial standards.

Start with essential expenses and resources

Illness, injury, caregiving, job changes, or a death in the family can change household income and costs. List essential monthly expenses, bills that could be reduced, and one-time obligations. Then identify resources that may be available, such as another household income, savings designated for emergencies, employer benefits, or insurance—without counting the same resource twice.

Do not assume a benefit will be available until you confirm eligibility, timing, duration, and amount with the program, employer, or insurer. Keep estimates separate from confirmed benefits.

Build a basic interruption checklist

  1. List housing, utilities, food, transportation, debt payments, care costs, and other essentials.
  2. Identify income sources and liquid savings the household could access, and note any restrictions or trade-offs.
  3. Review workplace sick leave, disability coverage, and other benefits; ask how eligibility and return-to-work rules work.
  4. Check insurance policy definitions, exclusions, waiting periods, benefit amounts, duration, and claim procedures.
  5. Record important account contacts and update emergency contacts and beneficiaries after major life changes.

Understand what different coverage is for

Life insurance generally pays a death benefit if the claim meets the contract terms; it is not a general replacement for a living insured person's paycheck. Disability income coverage, when available, may replace part of income under its own definitions and conditions. An accelerated death benefit rider may provide an early payment from a life policy after a qualifying event, which can reduce what remains for beneficiaries. These products are not interchangeable.

Learn more about living-benefit riders and life insurance types, or use the income section of the family protection guide for a broader planning worksheet.

Questions for an employer or insurer

  • What event qualifies, and how does the policy define disability or inability to work?
  • Is there a waiting period before benefits can begin, and how long could payments continue?
  • How are existing income, other benefits, or work activity treated when calculating a payment?
  • Can coverage change if employment ends, and what deadlines apply to continuation or conversion?
  • What documentation is needed to make and maintain a claim?

Benefit availability depends on the specific program, policy, insurer, employer, and applicable law. Confirm current terms directly and seek qualified tax, legal, or benefits guidance when needed.
